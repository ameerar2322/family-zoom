Prepared for Deacon Kenneth L. Radcliffe

The Isaiah Project Growth Plan

How to get more people to the website, sell more copies of Applying Alcoholics Anonymous Principles to the Disease of Racism, and fill the Recovery Room.

Based on a review of theisaiahproject.name, the book’s store listings, the podcast, and Deacon Radcliffe’s public profile. September 2026.

The bottom line

  1. The book is hard to buy. The order page has a $3.99 form with no visible way to pay, and it never links to the $15.99 paperback on Amazon and Walmart. People who want the book are getting lost at the last step.
  2. Nobody is being invited back. There is no email signup, no free sample, and no social links, so a visitor who isn’t ready to buy today leaves and never hears from the Isaiah Project again.
  3. The strongest story is buried. A 47-year Harlem deacon, 20-year Rikers chaplain, and certified addiction counselor wrote this book in 2012, years before the better-known “Racists Anonymous” groups got national press. The site doesn’t say any of that up front.

Real credentialsPermanent Deacon, Archdiocese of New York, for over four decades. Retired Administrative Chaplain, NYC Department of Correction (Rikers Island, Manhattan Detention Complex). CASAC since 2018 and a certified Recovery Coach.
Big-name endorsementsCardinal Timothy Dolan (“A Good Read”), Dr. Barbara C. Wallace of Teachers College, Columbia (“a vital tool for healing”), and Sandy Bernabei, LCSW.

Programs, not just a bookThe Recovery Room, Let Our Healing Begin, Dream Makers, and the St. Dismas Re-entry Project give people more ways to connect than a book alone.

What’s holding sales and traffic back

Fix now

The order page doesn’t clearly take payment

The e-book page shows a name and email form with a “E Book - $3.99” checkbox, but no PayPal, card, or checkout button is visible and the page never names the book. A buyer can’t tell what happens after they hit submit. There is also no link to the paperback, which sells for $15.99 at Amazon and Walmart.

Fix now

The Recovery Room requires the book, but doesn’t sell it

People must read the book to join the Recovery Room, yet the programs page doesn’t link to buying it. Every interested person should be one tap away from the book.

Fix now

A menu item opens a bare image

“If Your Brother Sins…” in the top menu opens a raw JPG file, which feels broken to a first-time visitor. This presentation was shared with Vatican officials by Cardinal Dolan. It deserves its own page with a short explanation.

Soon

The calendar says “no upcoming events”

An empty calendar tells visitors the organization is quiet. Either list the Recovery Room sessions and talks, even if they are “by appointment,” or remove Calendar from the menu until there are events.

Soon

No way to stay in touch

No newsletter, no free chapter, no social media links. Most people need to hear about a book several times before they buy. Without an email list, the site gets one chance per visitor.

Soon

The podcast lives on someone else’s site

There are two episodes, both guest appearances on the “Alma Am I Racist?” podcast, with no dates and no Apple or Spotify links. They are good proof that he is interview-ready, but they aren’t a podcast of his own yet.

Soon

No reviews anywhere

The book shows 0 ratings on Goodreads and Walmart. Buyers look for reviews first. The endorsements on the order page are strong but don’t appear on the store listings.

Later

The book is listed under “Health & Fitness”

Store categories decide who stumbles onto a book. Adding categories like Religion, Christian Life, Social Issues, and Recovery would put it in front of the readers most likely to want it.

Later

A personal Gmail as the only contact

An address like ken@theisaiahproject.name, which costs little or nothing with the domain he already owns, looks more established to parishes, schools, and event organizers.

One gap in this review: we could not open a preview of the book itself, and we don’t have the site’s visitor numbers. Adding free Google Analytics is step one of measuring any of this.

The plan

Ordered so the cheapest, fastest fixes come first. Nothing here needs a big budget.

Weeks 1 to 2

Make it easy to buy

  • Replace the order form with a real checkout. A PayPal or Stripe “Buy Now” button works on WordPress in an afternoon, and the e-book downloads automatically after payment.
  • Add a second button: “Prefer paperback? Buy on Amazon.”
  • Put a “Get the book” button on every page, including Programs and About.
  • Turn “If Your Brother Sins…” into a real page, and fix or hide the empty calendar.
  • Move the endorsements and his credentials to the homepage, right under the book.
  • Install Google Analytics and Google Search Console, both free, to see where visitors come from.
Weeks 2 to 4

Give people a reason to come back

  • Create one free “lead magnet”: a 2-page PDF called “The 12 Steps of Racism Recovery” or the book’s first chapter. Visitors get it in exchange for their email.
  • Set up a free email tool (Mailchimp or MailerLite) that sends a short welcome series: day 1 the free PDF, day 3 his story from Rikers to the Recovery Room, day 7 an invitation to buy the book or join a session.
  • Send one short email a month after that: a reflection on one Step, an upcoming talk, a reader’s story.
  • Ask 20 people who have read the book to leave an honest review on Amazon and Goodreads. Reviews are the single biggest factor on store pages.
Months 2 to 3

Sell to groups, not just individuals

  • Create a Parish & Group Study Kit: the e-book plus a simple 12-week facilitator guide with discussion questions for each Step. Sell it to parishes, schools, and recovery programs, or bundle 10 paperbacks at a group price. One parish order can equal a month of single sales.
  • Offer talks and workshops through a “Book Deacon Radcliffe” page with a simple request form. Honoraria and book tables at talks usually outsell a website.
  • Add a Donate button. As a 501(c)(3), the Isaiah Project can accept tax-deductible gifts to keep the Recovery Room free.
  • Run the Recovery Room on Zoom on a set night each month, so people anywhere can join, and list it on the calendar.
Months 3 to 6

Get the word out

  • Be a podcast guest. The two “Alma Am I Racist?” episodes prove he interviews well. Pitch Catholic, Black church, recovery, and social work podcasts, one a week. Each appearance sends listeners to the free PDF.
  • Short videos. One-minute clips of him explaining a single Step as it applies to racism, posted to YouTube, Facebook, and Instagram. A phone and good light are enough.
  • Articles on the site that answer what people search for: “Is racism a mental illness?”, “12 steps for racism recovery”, “faith-based racial healing group.” Each one links to the book.
  • Time it to the calendar. Plan pushes for MLK Day in January, Black History Month in February, and Juneteenth, when churches and schools look for speakers and study material.
  • Pitch the “first” story to Catholic New York, the Amsterdam News, and Harlem outlets: a Harlem deacon and former Rikers chaplain who applied the 12 Steps to racism back in 2012.
  • Consider an audiobook read in his own voice. At 58 pages it is short enough to record in a weekend, and audio reaches readers who don’t buy print.

How to tell it’s working

  • Website visitors per month, and where they came from
  • Email signups per month (a good early goal is 50)
  • Book sales across the site, Amazon, and group orders
  • Number of reviews (goal: 20 in the first 90 days)
  • Talks booked and Recovery Room attendance
